ABOUT THIS EXPERIMENT

Blip Arcade is an experiment in making software together with machines. Every game, every line of code and every pixel of the interface was made in conversation with Claude (Anthropic), Codex (OpenAI) and GitHub Copilot (GitHub). The human brought the ideas; the machines did most of the typing.

The question behind it: how far can a conversation go before it becomes a piece of software? Quite far, as it turns out.

► A tribute, not a copy

Blip Arcade is a tribute to the golden age of arcade games: Pong, Breakout, Snake, Space Invaders, 1942 and the many games they inspired. The studios and engineers who made the originals shaped the whole history of interactive entertainment.

The games here are new works inspired by those classics. They contain no code, graphics or sound from the originals; everything was written from scratch for this project.

Blip Arcade is strictly non-commercial. It charges no admission, serves no advertising and keeps nothing about you but the name you enter on a high-score table. It exists as a technical and creative experiment, and as a small thank-you to the people who invented the medium.

► Add a game

Want to add a game? Pull requests are welcome at github.com/jacobandresen/blip. The Blip library takes care of the Macroquad and Rust plumbing, so you can concentrate on the game itself.

For inspiration, look to the golden age of arcade video games (roughly 1978–1986), which produced hundreds of titles worth revisiting: Asteroids, Centipede, Frogger, Pac-Man, Donkey Kong, Tempest and many more.

Please respect copyright. Reimplement the mechanics and the style from scratch, and never copy proprietary code, graphics or audio. Original names are trademarked, so give your game a title of its own, as this project does: Rally for Pong, Serpent for Snake, Raider for 1942. When in doubt, make it a tribute, not a reproduction.

► Build with Blip

Making an arcade game of your own? The Blip API has illustrated examples from these games and a quick start for working with an agent such as Claude Code or Codex. Pick a reference game, describe what you want to play, and build from there.
