HOW TO PLAY ON PC AND MOBILE

► Keyboard

Every game runs on the keyboard. Each one also prints its own key hints on its card here and on its title screen, but the layout is the same across the arcade:

Move / aim←↑↓→ or WASD
Button 1 — fire / launch / serveSpace
Button 2 — second actionZ (Meteors hyperspace only)
Insert coin5 or C

Per game: Serpent steers with WASD / arrows. Bouncer, Defender and Raider move with A/D or the arrows and fire with Space. Meteors rotates with A/D, thrusts with W, fires with Space and jumps to hyperspace with Z. Rally is two-player on one keyboard: player 1 uses W/S or ↑/↓, player 2 uses I/K, and Space serves.

► Gamepad

A connected controller is picked up automatically. The left stick or D-pad moves, the bottom face button (A / ×) is Button 1, and the right face button (B / ◯) is Button 2. The on-screen stick and buttons light up to mirror whatever the pad is doing.

► Touch — phone & tablet

On a touch screen the cabinet's control deck sits along the bottom of every game:

Joystick (left)Drag the ball-top in any of 8 directions to move or aim. Let go to centre it.
Fire buttons (right)Tap for Button 1; a second button appears for games that use one (Meteors).
Coin slotThe COINS panel in the top-right corner. Tap it to drop a credit — up to 5 held at once.

The deck also animates under keyboard and gamepad input, so on a laptop it doubles as a live read-out of what is being pressed.

► The dial — Rally only

Rally puts away the joystick and gives each player a rotary dial instead — player 1 on the left, player 2 on the right. Twist a dial with your thumb like a real knob and your bat slides with it; spin it faster and the bat moves faster. On the title screen, tap the left dial to start a 1-player game against the computer, or the right dial for two players.

Why a dial? The original Pong (Atari, 1972) was played exactly this way. Allan Alcorn built it as a warm-up exercise set by Nolan Bushnell, and wired each player's bat to a single potentiometer knob mounted in the cabinet — you turned it to slide your paddle up and down, and that was the whole control scheme. The first machine, dropped into Andy Capp's Tavern in Sunnyvale, jammed within days because its coin box had overflowed. The dial here is a small tribute to that knob.
